# Unanswered human decisions

Apply the shared [execution-mode policy](execution-modes.md): make defensible
choices within delegated scope and record their rationale. This contract applies
when necessary human knowledge, judgment or authority remains unavailable; it
creates no blanket plan approval or additional permission checkpoint.

## Record and ask

When a role says a human decision is required, make it an explicit outstanding
question rather than only a known limitation. In its ordinary immutable attempt
artifact, record the item and stable question ID, precise question, relevant
facts/evidence, options and recommendation when supportable, why the model cannot
settle it, and the affected work or completion claim. Name the blocked milestone
and any independent work that can continue. Preserve the unresolved question and
its artifact reference through handoffs and resumes using existing artifact_paths;
no new canonical field or helper flag is needed.

Present that question directly, with enough context to answer it. A required
product decision takes precedence over a secondary presentation preference in
interactive question cards and the closing report. If several decisions genuinely
need answers, keep their identifiers separate; do not let one card stand in for
all of them. Disclosure, silence, an opened PR and a list of limitations are not
answers. If no answer arrives, retain the wait for affected work. At the actual
stop, release ownership with that wait saved.

A finding discovered during review or PR follow-up can create this wait too.
Route a necessary product answer to plan/waiting_for_human/factory-plan or an
architectural answer to design/waiting_for_human/factory-design, with the question
and evidence in the next action. A review-specific factual dispute retains its
review wait. Preserve existing code/PR references, accepted checks, fix counts,
other decisions and all earlier artifacts; do not restart completed work or
manufacture cross-repository authority.

Independent authorized work may proceed when it does not depend on the answer.
An intermediate PR may be useful, but its existence does not settle the question
or establish that the requested capability is complete. Save the appropriate
human wait before ending the invocation, even if the report also describes that
PR. Do not call the affected capability ready while its required decision remains
unresolved. A harmless disclosed limitation that needs no human choice is not a
new wait.

## Associate answers and preserve what remains

Before interpreting a reply, read the current question records and their evidence.
An answer naming the item/question, or an unambiguous answer to its sole pending
question, can settle it. With several questions, associate each part of the reply
with the choice it actually answers. Resolve only those choices; retain the others
and ask only the precise remaining question. For example, “keep the compact link”
answers a control-style question, not whether an app playback gap is acceptable.
A host card dismissed by an incoming message is not an answer or refusal by itself.

Record the exact direct response and observed provenance, resulting decision and
implications in a new attempt artifact; retain the old question/options/evidence.
Do not infer acceptance from a later unrelated instruction. New evidence or an
applicable explicit delegation may instead settle the question; explain what
changed and label an agent decision as such, never as a human answer. Existing
scope restrictions and unavailable authority remain binding.

Every later handoff/report must carry the unresolved IDs and affected milestone,
or cite the actual resolution artifact. Before a readiness/completion save, the
orchestrator checks that no still-required question was lost between roles. If a
question remains, preserve its wait and next action instead of converting it to
a follow-up or claiming done. Do not repeatedly save an unchanged wait or demand
that the user repeat an already recorded answer. A resolved product decision does
not itself authorize implementation, publication or external messages.
